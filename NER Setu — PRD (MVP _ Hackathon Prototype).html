Status legend used throughout: 🟢 REAL (actually implemented and working) · 🟡 SIMULATED (realistic generated data, clearly labelled in UI) · 🔴 NOT BUILT (future only). 2. Problem Definition

Real-world problem. Essential goods (medicines, food grain, fuel, produce, construction material) move on a thin network of hill highways (e.g., NH-6, NH-37, NH-2, NH-8 corridors). A single blocked segment can isolate a district for days because there is often no parallel road.

Why NER is hard.

  • Terrain: steep, geologically young hills; many single-lane sections; long detours (a 20 km blockage can mean 150+ km detour).
  • Rainfall: among the heaviest in the world (Meghalaya, Assam hills, Mizoram); monsoon saturates slopes.
  • Landslides/floods: slope failures on cut hillsides; Brahmaputra/Barak/Barak tributary flooding cuts valley roads.
  • Infrastructure: damaged roads, weight-limited or aging bridges, few alternates.
  • Connectivity: patchy mobile coverage in valleys/hills → delayed incident information.

Current gaps.

  • Blockage information travels by phone calls, WhatsApp and word of mouth, not to a common map.
  • No shared view linking weather → road risk → vehicles in transit.
  • Field reports are lost when the officer is offline.
  • Rerouting is a driver's guess.

Consequences of delays. Medicine stock-outs (cold-chain break), food and fuel shortages, missed harvest markets (produce spoils), stalled construction, delayed emergency response, driver safety risk.

Why map/navigation apps alone are insufficient. Consumer navigation optimises time on currently known traffic. It does not (a) forecast disruption from rainfall/terrain, (b) accept authority-verified blockages and photos, (c) treat medicine trucks as priority cargo, (d) give administrators district-level connectivity views, or (e) work offline for reporting.

5. Core User Journeys

J1 — Authority monitors road accessibility

  1. Logs in → lands on Dashboard (KPI cards + map).
  2. Sees districts shaded by connectivity score; clicks a low-score district.
  3. Map zooms; roads coloured green/amber/red/grey. Clicks a red segment.
  4. Side panel: status, risk score, contributing factors (rain, slope, reports), last update, linked incidents/photos.
  5. Filters to "Critical corridors only"; exports snapshot (optional).

J2 — Vehicle with medicines meets a high-risk route

  1. Operator's vehicle AS-01-MED-4521 is en route (simulated GPS every 5 s).
  2. Backend evaluates each vehicle's remaining route edges every 60 s.
  3. A downstream edge's risk crosses 0.70 within 20 km ahead.
  4. Vehicle marker turns amber; delivery flagged At Risk; alert generated (HIGH) for operator + authority.
  5. Driver app shows banner: "High-risk stretch ahead — reroute suggested".

J3 — System predicts a possible disruption

  1. Weather job fetches rainfall + 48h forecast per segment (every 30 min).
  2. Feature builder combines weather + static terrain + history + recent reports.
  3. ML service returns risk_score, category, predicted disruption type, confidence.
  4. Segment status updates (e.g., Accessible → Risky); RiskPrediction stored.
  5. Dashboard risk layer refreshes via Socket.IO; MEDIUM/HIGH alert raised if threshold crossed.

J4 — System recommends an alternative route

  1. Trigger: route risk over threshold, or blocked edge on active route.
  2. Routing engine recomputes with current edge weights (blocked = removed).
  3. Returns up to 3 options: Fastest, Safest, Balanced with distance, ETA, extra delay vs. original, max risk on route.
  4. Operator/driver taps Accept; vehicle's route updates; ETA recalculated; audit log written.

J5 — Field officer reports a blockage (online)

  1. Opens PWA → big Report Incident button.
  2. GPS auto-captured (accuracy shown); picks type "Landslide – road blocked".
  3. Takes photo (auto-compressed); optional short note (or voice-to-text later).
  4. Submit → 201 → toast "Report sent". Incident appears on authority map in <3 s.
  5. Nearest road segment set to Blocked (unverified) → alert raised.

J6 — Officer has no internet

  1. Offline banner shows; officer still creates report.
  2. Report + compressed photo saved in IndexedDB with client_uuid, status PENDING.
  3. Queue count badge ("2 waiting to sync").
  4. Connectivity returns → automatic sync (online event / Background Sync).
  5. Server dedupes by client_uuid; status → SYNCED; original captured_at timestamp is preserved.

J7 — Authority responds to a critical alert

  1. Push + in-app toast + red badge: "CRITICAL: NH-6 Segment KH-07 blocked".
  2. Opens alert → sees map, photo, affected vehicles/deliveries, suggested reroutes.
  3. Actions: Acknowledge, Verify incident, Mark emergency corridor, Notify operators.
  4. Later marks Resolved when a clearance report arrives; road status reverts through engine.

C. Road Accessibility Monitoring

Status Determined by (evaluated in this priority order)
Blocked (1) Authority-verified blocking incident active; or (2) unverified blocking report from ≥1 trusted source and risk ≥ 0.7; or (3) admin manual override. Auto-expires to Unknown after 12 h without confirmation.
Unknown No weather/prediction update > 3 h or no data at all for the segment (stale-data rule).

segment.status is recomputed by a scheduled job (every 5 min) and immediately on new incident / override. Status history is stored (for trend charts).

D. Weather Intelligence

  • Source: Open-Meteo API (free, no key) 🟢 real. IMD API integration = future.
  • Sampling: one query per weather cell (~0.25° grid, ~25–40 cells for demo), not per road; segments inherit the nearest cell. Cache 30 min in memory/Redis (or Mongo TTL).
  • Fields tracked: precipitation (mm/h), 24h and 72h cumulative rainfall, 48h forecast precipitation, wind gusts, weather code, derived weather_severity (0–3).
  • Severe weather warning rule (derived, not official IMD alert): 24h rain > 115 mm → "Heavy", > 204 mm → "Extreme" (aligned with IMD rainfall categories); label as derived.
  • Effect on risk: rainfall features feed the ML model (Section 7), and a deterministic weather multiplier is applied at routing time (Section 8).
  • Demo hook: POST /api/admin/scenario/weather can override a cell's rainfall (clearly marked "SIMULATED WEATHER" banner).

E. AI/ML Disruption Prediction (prototype)

  • Model: Random Forest classifier (multi-class) + calibrated probabilities. Trained on simulated dataset (Section 20).
  • Inputs: rainfall (1h/24h/72h), forecast rain 24h, road condition, slope, elevation, river distance, flood-prone flag, historical incident count (per 5 yrs), days since last incident, traffic level, previous landslide flag, road width, bridge count, recent field reports (count within 6h/5 km).
  • Outputs:
{ "risk_score": 0.78, "risk_category": "HIGH",
  "predicted_disruption_type": "LANDSLIDE_RISK",
  "confidence": 0.71, "top_factors": ["rain_24h_mm","slope_deg","recent_reports_5km"],
  "model_version": "rf-sim-0.1", "data_mode": "SIMULATED_TRAINING" }
  • risk_score = expected severity: P(medium)×0.5 + P(high)×1.0 (+ small weight for disruption class), clipped 0–1.
  • Disruption types: NONE, LANDSLIDE_RISK, FLOOD_RISK, ROAD_DAMAGE_RISK, CONGESTION_RISK.
  • Mandatory disclaimer in UI: "Prototype risk indicator based on simulated training data. Not a scientific landslide forecast."
  • Confidence: max class probability, reduced when input features are missing/imputed or weather data is stale.

F. Route Optimization Engine

  • Modes: Shortest (distance), Fastest (time), Safest (risk-dominant), Balanced (default), Emergency (safest, ignores traffic, allows Risky only if no alternative).
  • Returns: geometry, distance km, ETA, extra_delay_min vs. original route, max/avg risk, list of risky/blocked edges avoided, alternative list (≤3).

J. Offline-First Support

  • Stack: Service Worker (Workbox) caches app shell + last road-status snapshot; IndexedDB (Dexie.js) stores report queue and blobs.
  • Offline incident creation: identical form; saved with sync_status=PENDING.
  • Queue: FIFO; each item retried with exponential back-off (5 s → 5 min), max 8 tries then FAILED (manual retry).
  • Auto-sync: on online event, app open, and Background Sync API where supported (fallback: polling).
  • Idempotency: server enforces unique client_uuid; repeated POST returns the original record (HTTP 200).
  • Conflict handling: server never overwrites; conflicting reports are stored as separate reports and clustered by proximity (100 m) + time (±30 min) + type. Cluster status derived by majority + reporter trust (Authority > Field officer > Driver). Authority verification is the tie-breaker.
  • Cached read data offline: last-known road status GeoJSON (with "as of hh:mm" label).

7. AI/ML Design

Dataset requirements. Real: road inventory (OSM), terrain (SRTM DEM 30 m → slope/elevation), rainfall (Open-Meteo archive, free), historical landslide inventory (NASA Global Landslide Catalog / GSI Bhukosh — sparse), incident labels from government logs (unavailable). Therefore: hybrid synthetic strategy (Section 20): real terrain/rainfall features + simulated labels from a documented rule-based generator with noise.

Preprocessing. Impute missing numerics with median + missing-indicator; clip outliers (rain > 500 mm/24h); categorical → one-hot (road_condition, traffic_level via ordinal); standardise only if using logistic regression; stratified split by segment_id group (GroupKFold, avoids leakage).

Feature engineering. Rolling 1h/24h/72h rainfall sums; rain intensity ratio (rain_24h / (rain_72h+1)); slope × rain interaction; days_since_last_incident; incidents_per_km_5y; river distance bucket; report_density_6h_5km; night/monsoon-season flags.

Model comparison

Model Pros Cons MVP fit
Logistic Regression Fast, interpretable, baseline Can't capture rain×slope interactions well Baseline only
Random Forest Handles non-linearity + mixed features, robust on small/noisy data, built-in feature importance, little tuning, easy predict_proba Larger model file, slightly less accurate than boosting on big data ✅ Selected
XGBoost Best accuracy on tabular data, handles missing values More hyper-parameters, easy to overfit a simulated dataset, extra dependency Optional upgrade

Why Random Forest: with simulated labels, over-tuning gains nothing; RF is stable, explainable (feature importance + per-prediction top factors), trains in seconds and works out-of-the-box with scikit-learn — ideal for a student team.

Training. 20k rows simulated; 80/20 grouped split; 5-fold CV; RandomForestClassifier(n_estimators=300, max_depth=12, class_weight="balanced", random_state=42); probability calibration via CalibratedClassifierCV(method="isotonic"). Label: risk_level ∈ {LOW, MEDIUM, HIGH} and disruption_type.

Evaluation. Macro-F1, per-class recall (HIGH recall prioritised), confusion matrix, ROC-AUC (one-vs-rest), Brier score/calibration curve, feature importance sanity check (rain and slope should dominate). Report all metrics as "on simulated data — validates pipeline, not real-world accuracy".

Prediction API (FastAPI). POST /predict (batch of segment feature vectors → list of outputs), GET /health, GET /model/info. Latency target < 300 ms for 400 segments (vectorised).

Retraining strategy. MVP: manual script train.py versioned model files (rf-sim-0.1.joblib). Future: monthly retrain when verified incident labels ≥ N; store predictions + eventual outcomes to measure real accuracy; shadow-deploy new model before promotion; drift monitoring on rainfall distribution.

8. Route Optimization Design

Graph. Nodes = intersections/towns/bridge endpoints; edges = road segments (directed if one-way). For the demo, use a curated OSM subgraph (~150 nodes / ~400 edges) for 2 corridors with genuine alternates. Real place names, simulated statuses. Built offline with osmnx → exported to JSON; loaded into memory in Node at startup (Mongo stores the canonical segments).

Edge attributes: length_km, free_flow_speed_kph (by road class/condition), risk_score (latest), status, weather_multiplier, traffic_factor, bridge_restriction.

Edge cost

travel_time_h = length_km / (free_flow_speed × traffic_speed_factor)
risk_penalty  = risk_score^γ × length_km × R          (γ = 2 → penalise high risk non-linearly)
cost = w_d × length_km + w_t × travel_time_h×60 + w_r × risk_penalty
if status == BLOCKED: cost = ∞ (edge removed)
if status == UNKNOWN: risk_score = max(risk_score, 0.4)  (be conservative)

Weight profiles

Mode w_d w_t w_r Notes
Shortest 1.0 0 0 ignores risk (except blocked)
Fastest 0 1.0 0.1
Balanced 0.2 1.0 1.5 default
Safest 0 0.3 4.0
Emergency 0 0.5 3.0 Risky edges allowed only if no other path

Why a safer route can be longer: risk penalty scales with risk²×length. A 60 km stretch at risk 0.8 adds 0.64×60×R — e.g., with w_r=1.5,R=50 → +2,880 cost units versus a clean 110 km detour costing ≈ +2,200 units (distance+time). The detour wins, and the UI shows the trade-off ("+50 km, +85 min, risk 0.18 vs 0.81").

Algorithm. Dijkstra (A* with haversine heuristic optional) in Node (ngraph.path or hand-rolled binary-heap Dijkstra; graph is small so <50 ms). Alternatives: penalty method — after the best route, multiply weights of its edges ×1.5 and re-run up to 2 times; keep routes with <70% overlap and ≤1.6× best cost. ETA: Σ travel_time × (1 + weather delay factor: light rain +5%, heavy +15%, extreme +30%) × traffic. Extra delay = ETA(new) − ETA(current route recomputed with current conditions), or vs. original planned ETA. Re-route triggers: edge on active route becomes Blocked, or risk ≥ 0.70 within next 30 km, or operator request. Rerouting is suggested, never forced (except demo auto-accept toggle).

13. Mobile / PWA Design (Field Officer)

  • Installable PWA (manifest + service worker); works on low-end Android Chrome.

  • Frontend: react-i18next + i18next-browser-languagedetector; namespaced JSON: /locales/{lng}/{common,dashboard,alerts,report,errors}.json; lazy-loaded per language; language stored in user profile + localStorage.

16. Security

  • JWT: access token 15 min (memory only), refresh token 7 days httpOnly + Secure + SameSite=Strict cookie, rotation on refresh.
  • RBAC: middleware authorize(...roles) + resource scoping (operator sees own fleet; field officer sees own district/reports); tested per endpoint.
  • Passwords: bcrypt cost 12; min 10 chars; lockout after 5 failures/15 min.
  • API validation: zod/joi schemas for every body/query; reject unknown fields.
  • Rate limiting: express-rate-limit — 100 req/min/IP global; 10/min login; 30/min field-report; GPS 1 per 3 s/vehicle.
  • Secure uploads: MIME sniffing (magic bytes) not extension, max 2 MB, ≤3 files, image re-encoding to strip EXIF except GPS-derived fields we store separately, random filenames, no public listing, virus-scan = future.
  • Sanitization: express-mongo-sanitize, xss-clean/DOMPurify for descriptions, escape output in React (default).
  • HTTPS: enforced via hosting (Vercel/Render); HSTS via helmet; strict CORS allow-list.
  • Audit logs: login, status overrides, incident verification, alert ack, user changes; append-only.
  • Location protection: vehicle positions only to owning operator + authorities; precision reduced for non-privileged roles (e.g., round to 1 km); GPS history TTL 7 days.
  • Secrets: environment variables; no keys in repo; .env.example only.

Generation strategy (labelled SIMULATED):

  1. Real inputs where free: segment geometry/class from OSM; slope/elevation from SRTM; river distance from OSM waterways; historical rainfall from Open-Meteo archive for the past 2–3 monsoon seasons.
  2. Synthetic hazard score with documented rules: z = 0.030·rain_24h + 0.010·rain_72h + 0.06·slope + 0.5·prev_landslide + 0.15·hist_incidents + 0.4·(road_condition==POOR) − 0.0008·river_dist·flood_flag_weight + 0.3·recent_reports + N(0, σ) Convert to risk_level via thresholds (approx. 70% LOW, 20% MEDIUM, 10% HIGH); disruption_type chosen by dominant term (slope+rain → landslide; river proximity+rain → flood; poor condition → road damage; traffic → congestion).
  3. Noise & realism: 10% label noise, missing values in 3% of cells, seasonal weighting (monsoon heavier), class imbalance kept.
  4. Publish generator script + seed + data card stating: "Labels are rule-generated; model performance demonstrates pipeline correctness, not real-world predictive skill."
  5. Path to real data: replace label column with verified incident logs (state disaster-management/PWD/BRO data, NASA Global Landslide Catalog) while keeping the same schema.

31. Final Product Definition

Product vision. A trusted, shared, real-time picture of road accessibility across North East India — so essential supplies reach remote communities, especially when disaster strikes.

MVP definition. A web dashboard + field PWA that: shows road status on a GIS map; ingests real weather; scores segment risk with a prototype ML model; recommends risk-aware alternative routes with ETA impact; tracks (simulated + real-capable) vehicles; raises tiered alerts; and accepts offline-capable geo-tagged field reports that update the dashboard.

Core differentiator. It connects forecast risk → route decision → ground truth in one loop, with an offline-first field channel built for NER's connectivity reality — unlike generic navigation apps.

Key technical innovation. Risk-aware graph routing where ML risk and verified field reports directly modify edge costs, plus a conflict-tolerant offline sync pipeline (idempotent, trust-weighted clustering).

Key AI innovation. Explainable, calibrated segment-level risk scoring (score + type + confidence + top factors) that combines weather, terrain, history and live human reports, with safe fallbacks and a clear retraining path to real labels — without claiming scientific landslide prediction.

Key social impact. Fewer stranded medicine and food shipments, faster emergency response, better planning for remote districts, and a channel for local voices (field officers) to inform decisions.

30-second pitch

"In North East India, one landslide can cut off a district — and the truck carrying medicines finds out only when it's stuck. NER Setu combines live weather, terrain and field reports to score road risk, alerts authorities, and reroutes vehicles before they're blocked. Field officers can report from areas with no network, and it syncs when they're back online. One dashboard, from prediction to delivery."

2-minute pitch

  1. Problem (25 s): Hill terrain, extreme rain, landslides and floods make NER logistics fragile. Blockage news travels by phone; navigation apps don't understand landslide risk; officers in dead zones can't report.
  2. Solution (40 s): NER Setu monitors every road segment as Accessible, Risky, Blocked or Unknown using real weather data, a prototype ML risk model and verified field reports. Risk-aware routing computes safer alternatives and shows the exact trade-off in km, minutes and risk. Vehicles are tracked live, and tiered alerts reach the right people in English or Hindi.
  3. Field-first design (25 s): A simple PWA with big buttons captures GPS and photos, works offline, and syncs automatically without duplicates.
  4. Honesty & path (20 s): Today's model is trained on simulated data and is a decision-support indicator, not a landslide forecast; GPS and government data are simulated. The architecture is ready for real data, sensors, satellite inputs and official integrations.
  5. Impact (10 s): Faster response, fewer stuck shipments, better planning for the districts that need it most.

5-minute demo story

"A truck of essential medicines leaves Guwahati for Aizawl. As monsoon rain intensifies over the Jaintia Hills, our model raises the risk on its route; the dashboard turns the segment amber-to-red and fires a critical alert. The routing engine offers a safer path — 52 km longer, 65 minutes slower, but with far lower risk — and the operator accepts. Then a field officer, offline on a hill road, reports a real landslide on another segment; when the phone reconnects the report syncs, the map updates, an authority verifies it, and the truck is re-routed again. The medicines arrive. One loop — predict, alert, reroute, report, update — connecting the ground to the control room." (Step-by-step screen guide: Section 21.)
